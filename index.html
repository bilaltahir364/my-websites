Project 1 of 4: AI agent

Regulation Reader: an AI agent that turns EU legal texts into a checked obligations register

Sustainability rules arrive as long amending directives that change other directives. This agent reads the official text, extracts every threshold, date and obligation with its article reference, cross-checks each one against a second source, and marks what a human still has to verify.

By Bilal Tahir, built with Claude. Demo run on Directive (EU) 2026/470 (Omnibus I), November 2026.

How the agent works

1. Ingest

Official text only

Starts from the Official Journal text, never a blog summary. Records the version date.

2. Extract

One row per rule

Every threshold, date, exemption and obligation, each tied to the article and the provision it amends.

3. Classify

Tag and group

Scope, deadline, exemption, obligation, review. Grouped by the directive being amended.

4. Cross-check

Second source

Looks for an independent source (Parliament, law firm, regulator) that states the same figure.

5. Human check

I decide

Single-source rows are flagged. I verify them in EUR-Lex before anything is published or advised.

Guardrails built into the instructions

What the agent must do

  • Cite the article and paragraph for every row, or drop the row.
  • Copy figures exactly as written (EUR 450 000 000 stays EUR 450 000 000).
  • Name the provision being amended, since amending acts rarely make sense alone.
  • State its source text and that text's date at the top of the output.

What the agent must not do

  • Fill gaps from memory or from older versions of the law.
  • Interpret who is in scope for a specific company. That is my judgement call.
  • Mark a row as confirmed without a second, independent source.
  • Give legal advice. The register is a working tool, not an opinion.

The core instruction (excerpt)

You are reading an EU legal act. Work only from the text provided. For every threshold, date, deadline, exemption, obligation and review clause: 1. Write one row: area | type | rule in plain English | exact value | article + paragraph | provision amended. 2. Copy numbers exactly as written. Never round, convert or infer. 3. If a value is unclear in the text, write UNCLEAR and quote the words (max 25). Then search for one independent source per row. - Same figure found: status = Confirmed, with the link. - Not found or different: status = Verify in EUR-Lex. Never mark it confirmed. Finish with: source text used, its date, and the rows a human must check.

What this run showed

Where the agent saved time

  • Mapped one amending act across four directives in a single pass.
  • Separated CSRD dates (transpose by 19 March 2027) from CSDDD dates (transpose by 26 July 2028), a common source of confusion.
  • Surfaced small but practical rules, such as the 1,000-employee value-chain cap, that summaries often skip.

Where human judgement stays

  • 10 of the 21 rows had no independent second source and are flagged for checking in EUR-Lex.
  • National transposition can add stricter rules; the Netherlands still has to transpose these changes.
  • Deciding what a rule means for one company (scope, timing, data requests) is analysis, not extraction.

Project 2 of 4: CSRD double materiality

Double materiality assessment: Nordvorm Packaging

A worked assessment under the revised ESRS (adopted 3 July 2026) for a fictional Dutch food-packaging manufacturer, connected to the PPWR, CBAM and EUDR obligations the company faces.

By Bilal Tahir, November 2026. Drafted with a double materiality agent; the judgement calls are mine.

The company and every score are invented to show the method. The standards, thresholds and regulatory dates are real as of 9 November 2026.

1. Company and scope

Nordvorm (fictional: Zwolle, 1,450 employees, €540m net turnover, plants in the Netherlands, Poland and Germany) makes moulded-fibre trays, coated paperboard cartons, PET trays and aluminium-foil lids for EU food brands.

  • CSRD: in scope from FY2027. It exceeds both Omnibus I thresholds (more than €450m turnover and more than 1,000 employees).
  • CSDDD: out of scope (below 5,000 employees and €1.5bn turnover), but large customers will send due-diligence questionnaires.
  • Value-chain cap: with more than 1,000 employees, Nordvorm is not a protected undertaking and must answer customers' data requests. Its own suppliers with 1,000 or fewer employees are protected.
  • PPWR: applies since 12 August 2026, including PFAS limits for food-contact packaging.
  • CBAM: about 2,000 t a year of imported aluminium foil exceeds the 50-tonne threshold; first declaration due 30 September 2027.

2. Method

Each impact, risk and opportunity (IRO) is scored 0 to 5 on both axes. Anything at 3.0 or above on either axis is material. ESRS sets no numeric threshold, so 3.0 is a disclosed company judgement.

Impact = (scale + scope + irremediability) / 3 × likelihood / 5
Financial = magnitude × likelihood / 5
  • Top-down first: topics clearly not material were screened out at topic level, as the revised ESRS allow.
  • Whole value chain: upstream (pulp, aluminium, PET), own operations, downstream (brands, consumers, end of life).
  • Non-material means not reported: the revised ESRS require non-material information to be left out.

  • Judgement call: PFAS rated financially material (4.0) despite an impact score of 2.4, because the PPWR ban removes products regardless of likelihood of harm.

Project 3 of 4: framework analysis

ESRS E1 and IFRS S2: one climate data set, two reports

A disclosure-by-disclosure map of the revised ESRS E1 (adopted 3 July 2026) against IFRS S2 (as amended December 2025) and the GHG Protocol. For each theme: where the two align, where they diverge, and which data a company should collect once to serve both.

By Bilal Tahir, November 2026. Mapping drafted with an AI agent, every row checked against the standards.

Start with the one difference that drives the rest

ESRS, under the CSRD

Double materiality

Report a topic if it is material for its impact on people and the environment, for its financial effect on the company, or both. Revised ESRS keep this principle but allow a top-down assessment at topic level, and non-material information must now be left out.

IFRS S1 and S2, from the ISSB

Financial materiality

Report what could reasonably be expected to affect the company's cash flows, access to finance or cost of capital. Built for investors. Adopted by 35 jurisdictions as of July 2026, including the UK through UK SRS.

So a climate impact that is material to society but not to cash flows belongs in an ESRS report and may be absent from an IFRS S2 report. On emissions data itself, the two are close, because both rest on the GHG Protocol.

What the case studies recommend

Give it an ownerA dedicated sustainability team or leader with real authority, not goals spread thinly across departments.
Measure firstMateriality assessment, baselines and KPIs. You can't set credible targets for something you can't measure.
Report transparentlyRegular, verified progress reports. Moved deadlines damage trust faster than slow progress does.
Tie it to payLink executive and employee incentives to specific, numerical ESG metrics.
Bring suppliers alongCodes of conduct, science-based targets and emissions reporting. Most of the footprint sits in Scope 3.
Measure people tooSocial and governance KPIs as well as climate ones: worker safety, diversity, human rights.
CollaboratePeers, governments, NGOs and investors speed up what no single organization can do alone.
