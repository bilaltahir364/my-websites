(01) — The perspective

Science is the starting point. Impact is the objective.

I bring a materials engineering foundation to questions that sit between technology, business and sustainability.

My work has ranged from developing cellulose-based materials for 3D printing to evaluating energy-storage materials and assessing the feasibility and impact of climate technologies. I enjoy making complex technical questions understandable, measurable and useful for decisions.

The thread connecting these experiences is an interest in how materials and emerging technologies can contribute to more resilient, resource-efficient systems — and how their potential can be evaluated beyond the laboratory.

P—01 · MSc RESEARCH BIOBASED MATERIALS / 3D PRINTING

Printing with cellulose-based materials

A six-month proof of concept exploring whether cellulose-based formulations could be developed into printable structures for luxury packaging applications.

22.5 → 47.5%
Printable solid content
18
Formulations compared

For my MSc thesis with Cellulose Valley and Chanel, I developed a printability score (4–20) to compare formulations on equal terms. I tested 11 ink/nozzle combinations to establish extrusion force limits and designed a shape-monitoring test across 10 formulations. Weight shrinkage fell from 14.9% to 8.8%. The work included formulation development, rheological and structural characterization, prototype delivery and presentation of results.

P—02 NANOMATERIALS / ENERGY STORAGE

Improving supercapacitor performance

Investigating how combining metal–organic frameworks with conductive materials can improve electrode performance for energy-storage applications.

1,007 F/g
Specific capacitance
115%
Retention after 1,500 cycles

BSc final-year research at GIKI: Ni–Co–Mn metal–organic frameworks were composited with polyaniline (PANI) and reduced graphene oxide (rGO). Characterization included XRD, SEM, EDS, cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The work explored electrode structure, morphology and electrochemical behaviour.

P—03 CLIMATE / CIRCULAR ECONOMY

Assessing the potential of agricultural residues

Exploring how agricultural by-products can become useful resources through biochar and biogas pathways, supported by data analysis and feasibility work.

At Planetive, I analysed agricultural-residue data for biochar and biogas R&D and co-wrote a techno-economic feasibility report as part of a 10-person team. My work also included research into carbon-market mechanisms and carbon-credit potential. The focus was on assessing technical pathways alongside their economic and climate relevance.
